# Singapore GST InvoiceNow requirement

Singapore is phasing in mandatory transmission of invoice data to IRAS over the **InvoiceNow**
network, which runs on Peppol. Every GST-registered business will be caught by April 2031.

The reason this matters now, and the single most useful thing to tell an owner:

> **Onboarding takes 3 to 12 months.** So the date that matters is not the mandate date, it is
> the mandate date minus a year. An owner whose date is April 2028 should be choosing software in
> 2027, not in 2028.

## 1. Scope check first

**Not GST-registered?** The requirement does not apply. It attaches to GST registration, not to
company size. Say so and stop, but note that registering for GST later brings this with it.

**GST-registered?** They are caught. The only question is when.

## 2. The phase-in

| From | Who |
|---|---|
| 1 May 2025 | voluntary early adoption (soft launch) |
| 1 Nov 2025 | newly incorporated companies registering for GST voluntarily |
| 1 Apr 2026 | all new voluntary GST registrants |
| **Apr 2028 to Apr 2031** | **progressively, all GST-registered businesses** |

The 2028 to 2031 extension was announced at the **MOF Committee of Supply Debate 2026**.

**Do not guess which wave a business falls into.** IRAS is notifying businesses registered before
2026 of their specific date by **mid-2026**. If the owner has not had a notice, point them at
IRAS's own calculator on the GST InvoiceNow Requirement page. A wrong date here causes either
wasted spend or a missed mandate, and both are expensive.

## 3. What onboarding actually involves

There are two paths and the right one depends on what they already run.

**Path A: they use accounting software.** Most SMEs.

1. **Check the software is accredited.** IMDA publishes a list of **InvoiceNow-Ready Solution
   Providers (IRSP)**. If their product is on it, most of the work is done. If it is not, this is
   the moment to change software, not later.
2. **Register in the SG Peppol Directory.** Through their IRSP or Access Point, using their
   **UEN**, to get a **Peppol ID**. This is the business's address on the network.
3. **Enable GST InvoiceNow submission** so invoices transmit to IRAS from the solution.

**Path B: they run an in-house or custom system.** Rare for an SME, common for a manufacturer or
anyone with a bespoke ERP.

1. **Engage an IMDA-accredited Access Point Provider (AP)** to connect to the network.
2. **Register in the SG Peppol Directory** via the AP, using the UEN, to obtain a Peppol ID.
3. **Confirm the AP has connected the solution to IRAS** and enabled the submission feature.

**Free options exist.** IMDA publishes a list of free InvoiceNow solution packages, and there is
free provision aimed at GST-registered and newly incorporated businesses. Raise this before
anyone assumes a licence purchase is required, particularly for a micro business.

## 4. What InvoiceNow is not

Owners conflate these constantly. Be explicit.

- **It is not filing.** InvoiceNow transmits **invoice data**. The **GST F5 return is still filed
  separately** via myTax Portal or the accounting product. Adopting InvoiceNow does not file
  anything for them.
- **It is not a bookkeeping upgrade.** It changes how invoice data reaches IRAS. It does not
  reconcile, categorise, or close their books.
- **It is not optional past their date**, and it is not a grant scheme. Do not conflate it with
  funding support, which changes independently.
- **A Peppol ID is not a GST number.** It is a network address tied to the UEN.

## 5. Guardrails (always)

1. **Never assert a specific mandate date for a specific business.** Give the phase table, then
   point at the IRAS notice or calculator. This is the highest-cost error in this skill.
2. **Never name a specific solution as "the one to buy."** Point at IMDA's accredited IRSP list,
   which is the authoritative and current source. Vendor accreditation changes.
3. **Work backwards from the date.** Whenever a date is known or estimated, state the
   3-to-12-month lead time and give the month they should start.
4. **Flag it during any accounting software decision.** If an owner is choosing or changing
   software for any reason, IRSP accreditation should be a selection criterion now, because
   changing twice costs more than choosing once.
5. **Say when something needs checking** rather than filling the gap.

## 6. Output

1. **Are they caught?** Yes or no, with the reason.
2. **When**, as the phase table plus how to get their actual date. Never a guessed date.
3. **Which path** (A or B) and the three steps for it.
4. **The month to start**, working back from their date by the lead time.
5. **Flagged**: anything assumed, and anything they need to confirm with IRAS, IMDA, or their
   provider.

## Not covered here

Say so rather than improvising: the Peppol technical specification (BIS Billing, TX1 document
format), accreditation as an IRSP or Access Point, cross-border Peppol exchange, and the detail of
what invoice fields IRAS requires. Each has its own IMDA or IRAS document. Name it and stop.


## Freshness check (do this before quoting any figure)

Every figure in this skill was **verified 2026-08-08**. Singapore rules change on announced
dates, and some changes are already scheduled:

- **CPF contribution rates change 1 Jan 2027** (already announced)
- **GST InvoiceNow** phases run to **April 2031**, and IRAS notifications continue through 2026
- **GST moved 8% to 9%** on 1 Jan 2024, so rate movement is recent precedent

**If today is more than about six months after the verification date, say so before you give a
number.** Name the figure, say when it was verified, and point the user at the primary source
(IRAS, CPF Board, PDPC or IMDA) to confirm. Then give the figure.

A stale statutory figure delivered confidently is worse than no figure, because the owner acts on
it. Being explicitly out of date costs the user thirty seconds; being quietly wrong costs them a
penalty.
