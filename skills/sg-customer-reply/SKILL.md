# Singapore customer reply

Draft something the owner can send. Apply these rules over any generic complaint-handling process.

## 1. The owner gate (the rule that matters most)

**Never commit the business to anything without the owner deciding it first.** In measured runs,
unguarded drafting promised refunds, instalments and credits the owner had not agreed to, in most
complaint replies. That is money given away by a draft.

Gate all of these:

| Never commit without approval | Instead |
|---|---|
| a refund, full or partial | "let me check what we can do and come back to you today" |
| a credit, voucher, or discount | present the option to the owner, not the customer |
| an instalment or payment plan | flag as a decision the owner must make |
| a replacement or redo at no charge | same |
| a delivery or completion date you cannot verify | give a date only if the owner confirms it |
| any admission of fault or liability | describe what happened, not who is to blame |

**Produce two things:** the draft reply with no commitment in it, and a short list of the decisions
the owner needs to make, each with a recommendation. The owner picks, then the line goes in.

## 2. PDPA: what you can and cannot put in a reply

The Personal Data Protection Act governs how the business handles personal data, and a customer
reply is a place it goes wrong quickly.

- **Never repeat another customer's personal data** in a reply, including in an explanation of
  what went wrong. "A delivery was misrouted" not "it went to Mr Tan at [address]".
- **Do not include more of the complainant's own data than the reply needs.** A public review
  response should contain no order numbers, phone numbers, or addresses. Move to a private channel
  to verify identity.
- **Never ask for NRIC** to verify a customer in a routine service interaction. Use an order
  number or the contact detail already on file.

**If the complaint is itself about a data breach**, this stops being a service reply and becomes a
notification question:

| Fact | Value |
|---|---|
| Notify PDPC | no later than **3 calendar days** after determining the breach is notifiable |
| Notifiable when | likely to result in **significant harm**, **or** affects **500 or more individuals** |
| Also required | assess expeditiously once there are credible grounds, and **document the assessment** |

Tell the owner to get advice. Do not draft a breach notification.

## 3. Platform disputes go through the platform

If the sale happened on Shopee, Lazada, Amazon, Grab, foodpanda or similar, the platform's own
dispute process governs the outcome, and a side agreement in chat can cost the seller their case.

- Reply to the customer, but **redirect the resolution into the platform's process**.
- Do not offer a direct refund outside the platform to make a dispute go away.
- Note for the owner that the platform's timelines and evidence rules apply, not the shop's.

## 4. Tone

Write in Singapore business English. Direct, courteous, no US idiom, no corporate padding.

- Acknowledge the specific problem in the first line. Not "we value your feedback".
- Say what happens next and when. A vague "we will look into it" reads as a brush-off.
- Match the channel: a WhatsApp reply is short, a review response is public and read by future
  customers, an email can carry detail.
- **A public review response is written for the people reading it later**, not only for the
  complainant. Keep it calm, factual, and short.

## 5. Guardrails (always)

1. **No invented facts.** Do not state what went wrong unless the owner told you. "We are checking
   what happened" is honest; a fabricated cause is not.
2. **No invented policy.** Do not cite a returns window, warranty or guarantee that you have not
   been given.
3. **No legal characterisation.** Not "you are not entitled to", not "under Singapore law". If the
   customer raises a legal claim, flag it for the owner.
4. **Flag anything you assumed** in a line the owner reads before sending.

## 6. Output

1. **The draft**, ready to send, with no commitment in it.
2. **Decisions for the owner**, each with a recommendation and the line to add if approved.
3. **Anything flagged**: assumptions, missing facts, or a reason to get advice.

## Not covered here

Say so rather than improvising: legal advice, contract interpretation, employment disputes,
drafting a PDPC breach notification, and anything where the customer has engaged a lawyer or the
Small Claims Tribunals. Name what is needed and stop.


## Freshness check (do this before quoting any figure)

Every figure in this skill was **verified 2026-08-08**. Singapore rules change on announced
dates, and some changes are already scheduled:

- **CPF contribution rates change 1 Jan 2027** (already announced)
- **GST InvoiceNow** phases run to **April 2031**, and IRAS notifications continue through 2026
- **GST moved 8% to 9%** on 1 Jan 2024, so rate movement is recent precedent

**If today is more than about six months after the verification date, say so before you give a
number.** Name the figure, say when it was verified, and point the user at the primary source
(IRAS, CPF Board, PDPC or IMDA) to confirm. Then give the figure.

A stale statutory figure delivered confidently is worse than no figure, because the owner acts on
it. Being explicitly out of date costs the user thirty seconds; being quietly wrong costs them a
penalty.
